\section{Formal Models and Assurance Boundaries}
\label{appendix:formal}

The executable definitions in \path{formal/} are authoritative for the
\emph{covered models}. They replace this appendix's former handwritten
state-machine specification; they do not specify or prove the entire Rust
node. A definition alone is not a proved property. The tables below identify
named results, their premises, implementation tests, and gaps. Short theorem
names are qualified by the namespace stated beside each table. The complete
statements and their definition closure, rather than a prose paraphrase, govern.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.23\textwidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.71\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Status & Meaning \\
\midrule
Proven (model) & A named theorem about handwritten Lean definitions, exported
in \path{formal/Theorems.lean}. Its hypotheses must hold. A counterexample is
a proved refutation of the specified claim. \\
Tested & Finite Rust/model agreement or a named Rust/oracle test. Passing DRT
is evidence of correspondence, never universal Rust equivalence. \\
Proven (extraction) & A named theorem about generated Lean in
\path{formal-aeneas/}, with translation and primitive-library trust separate
from the handwritten model. \\
Trusted & An explicit premise or opaque dependency: for example cryptographic
binding, a clock reading, or a storage/encoding correspondence. \\
Not covered & No applicable proof or test boundary in this map establishes
the requested property. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A component may have several statuses for different claims. Both axiom
allowlists currently contain only \path{propext}, \path{Classical.choice},
and \path{Quot.sound}. There is no custom hash axiom: lottery hashes are
parameters of \path{Botho.Lottery.Prims}. Quantification over arbitrary
functions proves only the stated structural properties; it does not prove
SHA-256, RandomX, or curve security.

The \path{formal-gate} build/verify split checks exported statements,
definition closures and axioms using the trusted verifier, separately from
the untrusted build. Lean's kernel and the pinned toolchain remain trusted.
The Aeneas \path{typecheck} job instead builds and gates together: it is
\emph{non-isolated}, not the \path{formal-gate} trust boundary. Its claims
also trust the pinned Charon/Aeneas translation and Aeneas library's Rust
primitive semantics. The two packages do not share a gate; one package's
proof does not upgrade the other.

\subsection{Reading the Test Boundary}

The registered DRT leg names below are those in
\path{formal-drt/src/main.rs}. Their adapters under \path{formal-drt/src/}
define typed inputs, constructive generators, comparisons and saved replays.
Tests cover those sampled domains, not all inputs or accepted histories.
The seed adapter uses a Lean SHA-256 implementation with startup vectors;
RandomX output is supplied as data, not recomputed or proved in Lean.
Quorum tests and the accepted-block oracle are separate from registered DRT.

As dated baseline evidence, Formal DRT run
\href{https://github.com/botho-project/botho/actions/runs/42447672210}{42447672210}
(job \path{128629947677}) used seed 1707, with 10,000 generated and compared
cases per registered leg, 166 saved replays, and no mismatches or shrink
attempts. Its checkout was \path{060aefa539a13af0666da6c0a10d140c029a97b7};
its tree \path{02f61ad14c1e82b4a1c19247905f87810631a49e} is the documentation
review baseline. The report and artifact digests are retained with issue
\#1716. This is historical finite evidence, not a new run of these document
changes. Their own CI has its own source identity.
