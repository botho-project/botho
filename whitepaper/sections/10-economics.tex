\section{Economic Analysis}
\label{sec:economics}

This section analyzes the game-theoretic properties of \Botho's economic
mechanisms and their long-term sustainability.

\subsection{Incentive Alignment}

\subsubsection{Miner Incentives}

Miners (block proposers) are incentivized to:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Include transactions}: Larger blocks have no disadvantage
    (fees are burned, not captured)
  \item \textbf{Maintain network health}: Block rewards depend on network
    value, not transaction ordering
  \item \textbf{Stay honest}: Invalid blocks are rejected by SCP
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Why fee burning works}:

Without fee capture, miners have no incentive to:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Reorder transactions (MEV elimination)
  \item Censor low-fee transactions beyond minimum
  \item Create artificial congestion
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{User Incentives}

Users are incentivized to:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Circulate rather than hoard}: Progressive fees and tail
    emission create holding costs
  \item \textbf{Maintain privacy}: Default privacy eliminates coordination
    problems
  \item \textbf{Run nodes}: Reduced resource requirements enable
    participation
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Validator Incentives}

SCP validators (consensus participants) are incentivized by:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Reputation}: Being in quorum slices requires trustworthiness
  \item \textbf{Self-interest}: Validators typically also hold currency
  \item \textbf{Community standing}: Non-economic incentives matter for
    infrastructure nodes
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Progressive Fee Analysis}

\subsubsection{Fee Distribution}

Under the cluster-based progressive fee system:

\begin{equation}
E[\text{fee}] = f_{\text{base}} \times \text{size} \times E[\text{cluster\_factor}]
\end{equation}

The expected cluster factor depends on wealth distribution:

\textit{Fees are the size term of the congestion-floor consensus minimum
(1 pico-BTH/byte $\times$ 4{,}096 bytes $\times$ factor), before the output
penalty and demurrage; $4{,}000$ pico-BTH $= 4\times10^{-9}$ BTH. Under
congestion the dynamic base multiplies these by up to $100\times$. Default

so at today's constants an ordinary wallet pays the same fee at every
percentile (pending \#1688; the relay minimum is not a consensus rule, \#1684).}

\subsubsection{Redistribution Flows}

The lottery routes fees from fee payers to the holders of randomly drawn
outputs:

\begin{equation}
\text{redistribution}_{\text{direct}} = 0.8 \times \sum_{\text{tx}} f_{\text{total}}
\end{equation}

Fee burning (20\%) reduces supply, which benefits all holders in
proportion to their holdings and so does not by itself change the
distribution:

\begin{equation}
\text{redistribution}_{\text{indirect}} = 0.2 \times \sum_{\text{tx}} f_{\text{total}}
\end{equation}

These are flows, not outcomes. Whether they reduce inequality depends on
who pays and who wins; at production parameters they do not measurably do so
(Section~\ref{sec:redistribution-status}).

\textbf{Lottery selection as built}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Winners are drawn uniformly among eligible outputs
    (\path{SelectionMode::Uniform}, \path{cluster-tax/src/lottery.rs});
    every eligible output is one ticket, regardless of value or cluster
    factor (Section~\ref{sec:monetary})
  \item A uniform per-output draw depends on UTXO count, which a holder
    controls. Its split resistance rests on the Path~C reward cap, whose
    premises the implementation does not meet, so splitting is profitable
    (\#1761, pending \#1682); coinbase and payout outputs are also free
    tickets (pending \#1690)
  \item Any progressivity therefore has to come from the intake side
    (demurrage, and the factor-scaled fee where it binds), not from the
    payout weight; at production parameters that intake does not produce a
    measurable reduction in inequality
    (Section~\ref{sec:redistribution-status})
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Anti-Hoarding Dynamics}

The combination of
\begin{itemize}
  \item Tail emission ($\sim$2\% annual supply increase)
  \item Progressive fees (wealth-based costs)
  \item Lottery redistribution (80\% of fees to random UTXOs)
  \item Fee burning (20\% deflationary pressure)
\end{itemize}

is designed to create net pressure toward circulation:

\begin{equation}
\text{holding\_cost} = \text{dilution} - \text{lottery\_income} + \text{opportunity\_cost}
\end{equation}

Lottery income is per eligible output, not tilted by cluster factor (as
built, Section~\ref{sec:monetary}). Splitting does not change cluster
attribution, but shedding attribution is not rate-bounded by consensus: tags
can be dropped at the cost of the class-downgrade charge (as built, pending
\#1687, \#1686, \#1685; Section~\ref{sec:tag-decay}).

\paragraph{Intuition: why a holding cost, and why a targeted one.}
A fixed-supply currency in the Bitcoin mold makes hoarding costless, indeed
rewarding: a coin left untouched keeps its full claim on a supply that will
never grow, so the rational move for the largest holders is to sit still and
let scarcity appreciate their stake. That is good for a store of value but
poor for a medium of exchange, and it concentrates wealth over time because
those best able to wait accrue the appreciation. A pure tail-emission design
(perpetual issuance, no offsetting drag) softens the deflation but does not
change the incentive: new coins accrue to whoever mines, and idle wealth is
diluted only uniformly, which large holders can hedge simply by holding a
proportional share of hashpower. In neither design does sitting on wealth cost
the holder anything relative to their peers.

\Botho's anti-hoarding design makes idle wealth carry a small,
\emph{cluster-scaled} holding cost while leaving circulating money alone. The
intent is that the $\text{holding\_cost}$ term above is a net drag for the
high-factor clusters that dominate the supply and positive-leaning for
ordinary factor-1 balances, which would recover much of their dilution
through lottery income: a continuous incentive to \emph{spend} rather than
accumulate, without penalizing the everyday user the way classical uniform
demurrage does. At production parameters that targeting does not hold:
lottery income tracks block production and output count rather than
holdings, and decayed or coinbase-lineage tags price nearly all value at
factor~1 within a decade (Section~\ref{sec:redistribution-status}). Historical demurrage currencies (Freigeld, the
Chiemgauer) taxed every balance equally and were rejected as user-hostile for
exactly this reason (Section~\ref{sec:related}); \Botho's contribution is to
aim the same circulation incentive only where concentration actually occurs,
using the cluster factor as the targeting signal
(Section~\ref{sec:economic-modeling}).

\subsection{Game-Theoretic Analysis}

\subsubsection{Miner Strategy}

\textbf{Proposition}: Honest mining is a Nash equilibrium.

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Consider miner $M$ with hashpower fraction $\alpha$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Honest strategy: Expected reward $\alpha \times R$ per block
  \item Withholding: Risk of block rejection by SCP (no benefit)
  \item Invalid blocks: Rejected, wasted computation
  \item Transaction censorship: No benefit (fees burned anyway)
\end{itemize}
No deviation improves expected payoff.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Validator Strategy}

\textbf{Proposition}: Honest validation is incentive-compatible for
economically invested validators.

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Validators typically hold currency. Byzantine behavior risks:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Network failure (total loss of holdings)
  \item Exclusion from quorum slices (loss of influence)
  \item Reputation damage (non-economic but real)
\end{itemize}
The expected loss exceeds any potential gain from misbehavior.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{User Strategy}

\textbf{Proposition}: Using the system as designed is individually rational.

Users face choices:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Privacy}: Mandatory, so no choice to make
  \item \textbf{Fee payment}: Required for transaction inclusion
  \item \textbf{Holding vs. spending}: Mild pressure toward spending,
    but not coercive
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Long-Term Sustainability}

\subsubsection{Security Budget}

The annual security budget (miner revenue) is:

\begin{equation}
\text{budget} = R_{\text{tail}} \times \text{blocks\_per\_year} \times \text{BTH\_price}
\end{equation}

With tail emission, this is guaranteed to be positive regardless of
transaction volume. This directly addresses the fee-only security concern:
fee-funded security is subject to instability and potential
underinvestment~\cite{budish2022,huberman2021}, whereas a perpetual emission
floor decouples the security budget from fee-market volatility.

\subsubsection{Network Effect}

Privacy creates positive network effects:

\begin{itemize}
  \item More users $\rightarrow$ larger anonymity sets
  \item Larger anonymity sets $\rightarrow$ better privacy
  \item Better privacy $\rightarrow$ more users
\end{itemize}

This creates a virtuous cycle encouraging adoption.

\subsubsection{Fee Market Stability}

Fee burning eliminates fee market volatility:

\begin{itemize}
  \item No bidding wars for block inclusion
  \item Predictable costs for users
  \item No miner incentive to manipulate fees
\end{itemize}

The minimum fee provides sufficient spam resistance while remaining
affordable.

\subsection{Quantitative Economic Modeling}
\label{sec:economic-modeling}

This section presents formal modeling and simulation results for \Botho's
economic mechanisms.

\subsubsection{Adversarial Redistribution Simulation}
\label{sec:gini-experiment}

An earlier agent-based simulation evaluated candidate redistribution
designs. It does not model the implemented mechanism and does not
demonstrate it (Section~\ref{sec:redistribution-status}). Every candidate
mechanism was tested twice: once with honest
participants, and once against a \emph{strategic whale} that splits its
position into 1{,}000 UTXOs and churns them periodically to maintain
lottery eligibility --- the optimal gaming strategy known at the time. A
mechanism's redistributive effect was only credited in the \emph{gamed}
equilibrium.

\textbf{Setup}: 100M BTH economy; 80 small holders (5\% of wealth, cluster
factor 1$\times$), 30 mid holders (25\%, 2$\times$), 9 honest whales (65\%,
6$\times$), 1 strategic whale (5\%, 6$\times$); initial Gini $G_0 = 0.77$.
The operating point routes 2.5\%/yr of supply from tail emission into the
lottery pool and applies 2\%/yr demurrage at factor 6, with the split-invariant
6:1 payout tilt. Design criterion: $\Delta G > 0.05$ versus the
no-redistribution baseline. Runs are deterministic given an RNG seed (the
simulation forks a \texttt{ChaCha20} stream over order-stable
\texttt{BTreeMap} state, so a seed fixes the run byte-for-byte). To show the
headline result is not an artefact of one lucky draw, the winning mechanism
was re-run across an eight-seed set --- \texttt{\{2953379877, 626626626, 1,
42, 12345, 271828183, 314159265, 161803398\}} --- and the bold row below
reports the \emph{mean $\pm$ 95\% confidence interval} (Student-$t$, $n=8$) of
the five-year $\Delta G$ against the no-redistribution baseline. Each seed is
reproducible via \texttt{cluster-tax-sim lottery-experiment -{}-blocks 7884000
-{}-seed <seed>}; the other (comparison-only) rows use the first seed.

\textbf{These levers are part of the specified protocol}, not analysis-only
add-ons. Emission-routing is the height-scheduled emission share paid into the
lottery pool (Section~\ref{sec:monetary}, ramping to a 50\% cap), and
demurrage is the cluster-scaled stock-level term on the consensus fee floor
(Section~\ref{sec:cluster-tags}). The payout lever is not: the
``cluster-tilted'' rows below simulate the retired value-weighted, tilted
draw, while the protocol draws winners uniformly
(\path{SelectionMode::Uniform}, \path{cluster-tax/src/lottery.rs}).
The table is therefore an earlier design's result, not a measurement of the
implemented mechanism. The row closest to the implemented draw is ``Uniform per UTXO
+ emission'', which inverts under gaming; the Path~C reward cap was meant to
prevent that inversion, and as built it does not bind (\#1682). The
code-faithful re-runs (\#1696 for M2 at production scale, \#1761 for splitting
under the implemented cap) are reported in
Section~\ref{sec:redistribution-status}.

Three structural results from that model:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Per-UTXO payouts invert under gaming.} The uniform lottery
    is the strongest redistributor in the honest world and the worst
    mechanism overall in the gamed one: the strategic whale captures the
    payout stream, growing from 5\% to 24\% of supply in five years while
    the Gini coefficient \emph{rises}. UTXO count is a free variable;
    nothing may be paid on it.
  \item \textbf{Emission with proportional payout is exactly Gini-neutral},
    confirming that dilution redistributed in proportion to wealth changes
    nothing --- the payout must be tilted.
  \item \textbf{Cluster-anchored mechanisms are split-invariant.} Under the
    cluster-tilted lottery the gamed run shows marginally \emph{more}
    redistribution than the honest run: the attacker's splitting and churn
    fees feed the lottery pool, while costing the attacker $\sim$19\% of its
    position over five years. Attacking the mechanism funds it.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Expected lottery income as built}. Under the uniform draw, a holder
with $k$ of the $\rho$ eligible outputs in the candidate set expects, per
block of payout $P$,
\begin{equation}
E[\text{income}] \approx P \cdot \frac{k}{\rho},
\end{equation}
independent of value and cluster factor. This depends on how a position is
divided across outputs, which is why the splitting premises (\#1682) matter.
The tilted formula of earlier drafts,
$P \cdot v(\phi_{\max}-\phi+1) / \sum_j v_j(\phi_{\max}-\phi_j+1)$, describes
the retired \texttt{ClusterWeighted} mode only.

\textbf{What the earlier model reported}. In that model, at its
parameters, the retired cluster-tilted design with emission routing and
demurrage lowered the Gini coefficient by $0.078 \pm 0.0002$ per five years
($\Delta G$ vs.\ the no-redistribution baseline; mean $\pm$ 95\% CI over the
eight seeds), and by $\approx 0.097$ in absolute terms ($0.77 \to 0.68$); the
gamed run gave $0.076$. The fee-only configuration (no emission routing, no
demurrage) gave $+0.02$/5yr: transaction fees are a consumption tax and
cannot by themselves touch idle wealth at realistic velocities. These
figures describe a design the protocol does not implement and are kept only
as a record of how the design was chosen. They are not a property of
\Botho.

\subsubsection{Status of the Redistribution Claim}
\label{sec:redistribution-status}

\textbf{Measurable inequality reduction is not demonstrated at production
parameters.} This restatement (2026-11-01) follows the operator ruling on
issues \#1727 and \#1682. It changes no constant and no consensus rule.

\textbf{What the protocol does, as specified.} The fee floor scales with a
coin's cluster factor ($1\times$--$6\times$). Cluster-scaled demurrage charges
high-factor coins in proportion to value and holding time, from the second
halving epoch on; factor-1 coins are exempt. The lottery pool receives 80\%
of fees (demurrage included) and a height-scheduled emission share, and pays four
outputs per block drawn uniformly from the unspent outputs aged 720 to
10{,}000 blocks. Cluster tags record each output's provenance; each coinbase
starts a fresh cluster. These descriptions stand.

\textbf{What is not demonstrated.} A code-faithful re-run (issue \#1696, PR
\#1790, \texttt{cluster-tax-sim m2-production}) uses the node's fee floor,
5\,s blocks, the mainnet emission and lottery-share ramp, the uniform draw
with payouts as new outputs, coinbase-lineage clusters and an optimal-decoy
mining whale, and compares each run against the same seed with the mechanism
switched off. The mechanism's own $\Delta G$ is between $-0.028$ and
$+0.001$ (three-seed means at 10 and 20 years) against the $0.05$ design
bar. Even at $1000\times$ the transaction rate it is at most $+0.019$. By
year 10, 100\% of top-decile value is priced below $1.5\times$. The measured
failure mechanisms are structural:
\begin{itemize}
  \item lottery tickets track hashpower and output count, because most
    eligible outputs are coinbases and payouts that copy their winner;
  \item frequent spenders lose their tickets, since a spend removes the
    spent outputs from the candidate window and new outputs need 720 blocks
    to become eligible;
  \item tag decay and coinbase-lineage clusters bring nearly all value to
    factor~1, so demurrage stops, and at high transaction density it
    disappears altogether;
  \item per-output splitting buys tickets (below).
\end{itemize}
Under egalitarian mining the mechanism is mildly regressive on average. The
record is \path{docs/research/m2-production-verdicts.md}.

\textbf{Lottery splitting is profitable.} A faithful-to-code re-run of the
splitting experiment (PR \#1761, \texttt{lottery-reward-cap-sweep
-{}-faithful}) finds splitting profitable at all 192 grid points: an extra
output costs far less than the ticket share it buys, and the Path~C reward
cap does not bind as built (issue \#1682). The record is
\path{docs/research/ct-compatible-lottery-selection.md}, \S9.1.1.

\textbf{What happens next.} A sim-only redesign study (issue \#1801) targets
the failure mechanisms above. Candidate directions include tickets weighted
by value, age or cluster factor instead of output count, and any candidate
must pass the production-fidelity harness of \#1790 and the splitting grid of
\#1761 before a constant or consensus rule moves. Raising the existing levers
(demurrage rate, lottery share, per-transaction floor) was considered and
rejected, because the failure is structural. Until the study concludes, the
redistribution mechanism should be read as specified and implemented, but not
as a demonstrated means of reducing inequality.

\subsubsection{Formal Nash Equilibrium Analysis}

We prove that honest participation constitutes a Nash equilibrium under
reasonable assumptions.

\begin{definition}[Miner Game]
The miner game $\Gamma_M = (N, S, u)$ consists of:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Players $N = \{1, \ldots, m\}$ (miners with hashpower $\alpha_i$)
  \item Strategy space $S_i = \{\text{honest}, \text{deviate}\}$
  \item Payoff $u_i(s) = R \cdot \alpha_i \cdot \mathbb{1}[\text{block accepted}]$
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Miner Nash Equilibrium]
In $\Gamma_M$, the strategy profile where all miners play honest is a Nash
equilibrium for any $\alpha_i < 0.5$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Consider miner $i$ with hashpower $\alpha_i$. Under honest strategy:
\[
u_i(\text{honest}, s_{-i}^*) = R \cdot \alpha_i \cdot P(\text{valid}) = R \cdot \alpha_i
\]

Under deviation (invalid block, withholding, or censorship):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Invalid block}: SCP rejects with probability 1.
    $u_i(\text{invalid}) = 0 < R \cdot \alpha_i$.
  \item \textbf{Block withholding}: Reduces probability of own block
    selection. $u_i(\text{withhold}) < R \cdot \alpha_i$.
  \item \textbf{Transaction censorship}: No payoff change (fees burned).
    $u_i(\text{censor}) = R \cdot \alpha_i = u_i(\text{honest})$.
\end{enumerate}

No deviation strictly improves payoff. Censorship is weakly dominated
due to reputation costs not modeled here.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Validator Coalition Game]
The coalition game $\Gamma_V = (N, v)$ consists of:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Validators $N$ with quorum slice structure $\mathcal{Q}$
  \item Characteristic function $v: 2^N \to \mathbb{R}$ where $v(S)$ is the
    value coalition $S$ can guarantee
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Coalition Resistance]
Under quorum intersection, no coalition $S$ with $|S| < f_{\text{threshold}}$
can guarantee positive deviation payoff.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
A deviating coalition must either:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Block consensus (requires breaking quorum intersection)
  \item Force different values (requires $> f$ Byzantine nodes)
\end{enumerate}
Both require coalition size exceeding Byzantine threshold. Below threshold,
$v(S) \leq 0$ for any deviating strategy.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Economic Attack Cost Analysis}

We quantify the cost of various economic attacks.

\textbf{51\% Attack Cost}.
Unlike pure PoW, \Botho requires both hashpower majority AND quorum corruption:

\begin{equation}
\text{Cost}_{51\%} = \text{Cost}_{\text{hashpower}} + \text{Cost}_{\text{quorum}}
\end{equation}

For hashpower (assuming \$0.05/kWh electricity, efficient mining):
\begin{equation}
\text{Cost}_{\text{hashpower}} = \frac{0.51 \cdot H_{\text{network}} \cdot P_{\text{unit}}}{E_{\text{unit}}} \cdot t_{\text{attack}}
\end{equation}

For quorum corruption (must compromise $\geq f + 1$ validators):
\begin{equation}
\text{Cost}_{\text{quorum}} \geq (f + 1) \cdot V_{\text{stake}} \cdot P_{\text{bribe}}
\end{equation}

where $P_{\text{bribe}}$ represents the fraction of stake validators require
to defect.

\textbf{Cluster Factor Manipulation}.
To reduce cluster factor from $f$ to $f'$:

\begin{equation}
\text{Cost}_{\text{cluster}} = \text{fee}(\text{mixing}) + \text{slippage} + \text{counterparty\_cost}
\end{equation}

Mixing requires acquiring low-cluster UTXOs through genuine trade. With
efficient markets, arbitrage prevents free cluster factor reduction.

\textbf{Lottery Grinding}.
The seed is bound to the parent block's proof of work (protocol 7.0.0,
Section~\ref{sec:security}, Lottery Grinding), so each new seed costs a PoW
solution. A miner that discards a solution forfeits its coinbase,
$(1-f_{\text{em}})\,R(h)$, not the full reward $R(h)$; with the lottery
emission share $f_{\text{em}}$ at its 50\% cap, that is half a reward against
a redirectable payout of up to one reward. The candidate set can also be
changed without new PoW through the parent block's transaction set (as
built, pending \#1689).

\subsection{Economic Risks}

\subsubsection{Low Adoption}

If adoption remains low:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Tail emission maintains miner incentive
  \item Progressive fees have minimal impact (few large holders)
  \item Network can persist indefinitely at low scale
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Mining Centralization}

Risks and mitigations:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Risk}: ASIC development concentrates mining
  \item \textbf{Mitigation}: CPU-friendly PoW (RandomX-based~\cite{randomx})
  \item \textbf{Risk}: Pool concentration
  \item \textbf{Mitigation}: Solo mining viable with tail emission
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Validator Cartel}

If validators collude:
\begin{itemize}
  \item SCP's tiered structure provides defense
  \item Community can adjust quorum slices
  \item Economic self-interest opposes cartels
\end{itemize}
